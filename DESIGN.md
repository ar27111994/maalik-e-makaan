## Overview

The look is a Pakistani landlord's **raseed book** made trustworthy on a phone: carbon-copy
cobalt ink on cool white paper, every amount in clear, even figures, the counterfoil kept for
the record. The one ornament is the **brass house nameplate** from the gate of a Pakistani
home, an engraved, honoured name, used only where a record deserves honour: a property's name,
a receipt's seal. Everything else is plain paper and ink, legible in sunlight on a ten-thousand-rupee
Android phone, in Urdu as naturally as in English.

## Colors

Cobalt carries the one action that matters on a screen; ink carries everything people read;
brass appears at most once per screen and never as text on its own. Status colours always
travel with an icon and a word, never alone.

- **Page (`bg`):** cool paper. Never cream or beige; the paper of a receipt book, not a menu.
- **Surface (`surface`):** cards, sheets and list groups sit on it.
- **Ink (`ink`):** text and icons. **Muted ink (`inkMuted`):** captions, dates, metadata.
- **Outline (`outline`):** field and control borders, at 3:1 or better. **Rule (`rule`):**
  decorative dividers only, never the only boundary of a control. In the high-contrast modes
  the rule stays at 3:1 or better against the page but at least 3:1 apart from the outline, so
  a disabled control inside its rule border never reads as an enabled outlined one.
- **Cobalt (`primary`):** the single filled action per view, links, selection, focus.
- **Brass (`brass`):** the nameplate border, the receipt seal, a featured property. Use
  `brassText` when brass must carry words.
- **Status:** `paid` (settled), `overdue` (past grace, errors), `dueSoon` (inside the reminder
  window, warnings), `credit` (tenant advance, information), each with a container and an
  on-container colour for chips and banners.

Dark and high-contrast modes keep the same roles (see `design/tokens/maalik.tokens.json`); every
pairing above is checked at build time: 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for controls, and 7:1 for text in
the high-contrast modes.

## Typography

- **Latin: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.** Designed for low vision: 0 and O, 1, l and I, 5 and S
  stay distinct, which matters for rupee amounts, CNICs and phone numbers read at a glance.
- **Urdu: Noto Sans Arabic.** A plain sans that reads easily on a phone (D-19): its lines never
  touch from 1.4 times its size up, its words sit centred in buttons and fields, and screens are
  shorter than in Nastaliq. It is set a size step larger than Latin, on lines about one and a
  half times its size; never squeeze it to fit.
- **The wordmark: Noto Nastaliq Urdu.** The brand name مالکِ مکان keeps Nastaliq where it stands
  alone as the wordmark: in the app, a screen that shows only the app's name (`MmPage.wordmark`);
  on the website, the header's brand name and the link-preview cards; the pitch deck's cover. It
  sits on a line twice its size for its tall letters. Nastaliq is never used for interface text,
  nor for the name inside a sentence.
- **Amounts** use the Latin face with tabular figures in both languages, so columns of rupees
  line up and a 1 never looks like a 7.
- **User data keeps its own script's face.** Latin-script data in the Urdu UI (a name typed in
  English, a CNIC) is set in Atkinson, not in the Urdu face's own Latin letters; Urdu letters in
  the English UI (a tenant's name typed in Urdu, the rupee word after an amount) fall back to
  the Urdu face, never to a platform face or empty boxes. The spaces between Urdu words and a
  Latin name are the Latin face's. The Latin face does not draw the narrow no-break space
  `intl` puts before AM and PM; it is shown as a no-break space.
- Roles: `amount` (one large figure per screen), `headline` (screen titles), `title` (section
  and card titles), `titleSmall`, `body` (default), `bodySmall`, `label` (buttons, chips),
  `caption` (dates and metadata; 12 px is the floor).
- Text scales to 200%: layouts reflow and wrap; nothing truncates a name or an amount silently.

## Layout

A 4 dp grid. Phones first, from 320 dp: one column, the primary action within thumb reach at
the bottom. At 600 dp a navigation rail replaces the bottom bar; at 840 dp lists and their
details sit side by side; reading columns stop at 720 dp. Mirror everything for Urdu (start and
end, never left and right): icons that point, progress and swipe directions flip; amounts stay
in Western order. Trends and charts run along time from the start edge, so trend arrows mirror
too. A minus sign stays on the left of the figures in Urdu as in English. Lists of money align
amounts to the end edge, and a value that moves under its label at large text keeps to that
edge, so a card's figures share one edge.

## Elevation & Depth

Paper does not float. Surfaces are separated by tone and spacing, not shadows: the page, cards
on it, inset wells for search and the receipt counterfoil. Shadow is reserved for things that
are truly on top: bottom sheets, dialogs, menus and the floating action button.

## Shapes

Radii step with size and purpose: `xs` 4 for badges and chips, `sm` 8 for buttons and fields,
`md` 12 for cards and list groups, `lg` 16 for sheets and dialogs, `full` for avatars. A
receipt's edge is straight with a perforation, not rounded.

## Components

- **Button:** one filled cobalt button per view for the main action; outlined for the
  alternative; text buttons for low-emphasis actions, and a text link whose label lines up
  with the words above it (its padding reaching into the margin) for the next step at the
  foot of a card. 48 dp tall; labels are verbs ("Record payment"), never "OK".
- **Destructive actions:** red is filled only at the point of no return. The button that
  starts removing or ending a record (in a sheet or on a screen: "Remove member", "Delete
  document", "End tenancy", "Void payment", and the "Delete" at the foot of a record's screen)
  is outlined and labelled in `overdue`, wherever it sits, so the same act always looks the same
  and a sheet keeps at most one filled button; it opens a confirmation (or a sheet asking why)
  whose confirm button, a verb that repeats the action, is the filled red one. Clearing a
  message from the inbox (a notification) removes no record and asks nothing: it stays a plain
  tertiary button. A flow built only for one destructive act (deleting the account) ends
  on a filled red button too. When "Cancel" would be ambiguous next to the action ("Cancel the
  repair"), the dialog's way out names itself ("Keep the repair").
- **Field:** labelled above, helper or error text below in words (not colour alone), 56 dp tall,
  currency fields show "Rs" and format as you type. A currency field keeps its amount against
  its currency word: "Rs" first at the start edge in English, "روپے" last at the end edge in
  Urdu, where Urdu amounts sit in lists and receipts; other fields start at the start edge.
  Words typed in the other language run in their own direction, decided by their first
  letter (an Urdu reminder typed in the English UI runs right to left), under a label in the
  UI's direction. A one-line field keeps them at the start edge of the form; a field of
  several lines starts every line at the start of the text's own direction, as `dir="auto"`
  does (the right edge for that Urdu reminder), so a paragraph never has a ragged start. A
  passage shown back (a note in a detail card) does the same.
- **Card:** a surface with 16 dp padding that groups one thing; cards never nest. A card or
  tile that only shows information is bounded by a `rule` hairline (or by tone alone); one you
  can tap is a control, so it is bounded by the `outline` colour, at 3:1 or better.
- **Status chip:** container colour, an icon and a word ("Paid", "Overdue 12 days"). A chip
  or a receipt's seal that holds an amount or a number keeps it whole wherever it fits; at
  200% text, where an amount and its currency word together are wider than the chip ("1,25,000
  روپے"), they may wrap between the figures and the word, and a receipt number after one of
  its dashes. The figures themselves never break. This is the one exception to the money-text
  rule below.
- **Count badge:** ink with paper figures, never a status colour: a count says how many things
  wait; the label beside it says what they are.
- **Money text:** tabular figures, rupee prefix, lakh/crore or Western grouping per setting;
  the currency and its figures are joined by a no-break space, so they never part at a line
  end; negative balances say "credit" in words, not a minus sign alone.
- **Nameplate:** the property name on a brass-bordered plate in property headers.
- **Receipt:** paper surface, perforated top edge, brass seal with the receipt number; the same
  layout prints to PDF.
- **Empty, error and loading states:** say what happened and the next step in one sentence,
  with one action; skeletons, not spinners, for lists.
- **Focus:** keyboard focus is a 2 dp ring in `focus`. Outlined and text controls and fields
  draw it on their edge. Filled controls (filled and icon buttons, the floating action button)
  draw it 2 dp outside, past a gap of whatever they sit on, so it shows on any fill, cobalt
  included; leave 4 dp around them so nothing covers it.
- **Disabled:** muted ink. A filled control turns to sunken paper inside a `rule` border, never
  cobalt at low opacity, so it stays readable in every mode.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Put one primary action on a screen and make it the thing the landlord came to do.
- Show who owes what and since when before anything else.
- Pair every status colour with an icon and a word, in both languages.
- Use start/end (`EdgeInsetsDirectional`, `AlignmentDirectional`) everywhere.

### Don't

- Don't use purple, indigo, gradients, glass, glows or neon: this is a ledger, not a launch page.
- Don't put the page on cream or beige, set headings in a serif, or reach for sage green.
- Don't add shadows to cards, coloured side stripes, cards inside cards, or big rounded icon
  tiles above headings.
- Don't write tiny tracked all-caps labels above sections.
- Don't show money in proportional figures, or let a rupee amount wrap mid-number (a chip may
  wrap an amount between its figures and its currency word, never inside the figures).
- Don't fill a red button that only opens a confirmation: outline it.
- Don't shrink or tighten Urdu to match English line heights.
- Don't signal paid, overdue or errors by colour alone.
