## How the calculator works

1. Count the days from the first day of the tenancy to the last day of that month, counting both.
2. Divide by the number of days in that month: 28, 29, 30 or 31.
3. Multiply the monthly rent by that share, and round once to the nearest rupee, with a half rounding up.

A tenancy that starts on the 1st pays the whole month. From the next month on, the full rent is due.

This is the rule the Maalik-e-Makaan app uses for the first month, and the calculator runs the same arithmetic, checked against the same test cases.

## Is there a legal rule?

We found no statute, court ruling or official guidance that says how to charge rent for part of a month. Charging by the actual days in the month is one convention; another counts every month as 30 days, which gives a slightly different figure in months that are not 30 days long. Whichever you use, write it into the agreement.

The laws we read leave the amount and the due date to the agreement: Punjab's [Rented Premises Act 2009](http://punjablaws.gov.pk/laws/498.html) expects the agreement to state the rent and its due date, and Sindh's [Rented Premises Ordinance 1979](https://www.sindhlaws.gov.pk/setup/publications_SindhCode/PUB-15-000484.pdf) makes rent due by the tenth of the following month when no date is agreed.
